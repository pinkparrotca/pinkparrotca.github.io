Origin · Minas Gerais, Brazil The Basics

Brazil's Most Golden Treasure.

The mango has been called the "King of Fruits" for good reason. Introduced to Brazil by Portuguese colonizers in the 17th century, the fruit found its paradise in the tropical soils and climate of South America, where it evolved into hundreds of unique Brazilian varieties.

Among them, the Ubá mango — native to the Minas Gerais region — stands out as one of the most celebrated. Small to medium in size, with a golden-yellow skin and a reddish blush when ripe, the Ubá is known for its intensely rich, sweet flavor and incredibly juicy flesh.

Scientific studies have confirmed that Ubá mango pulp is exceptionally rich in bioactive compounds — including mangiferin, kaempferol, and quercetin — powerful antioxidants that support immunity, reduce inflammation, and protect cells from oxidative stress.

Nutritional Benefits

The Most Complete Tropical Superfruit.

Brazilian mango packs an extraordinary range of vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants. Here's what makes it one of the most nutritionally complete fruits on the planet.

Vitamin A Powerhouse

The golden color of Brazilian mango signals an exceptionally high beta-carotene content — converted by your body into Vitamin A, which supports vision, skin health, and immune function.

Immune-Boosting Vitamin C

Rich in Vitamin C, which stimulates collagen production, enhances iron absorption, and provides powerful protection against free radical damage.

Natural Energy

Mango's natural sugars paired with B-vitamins provide clean, sustained energy without the crash — perfect pre or post-workout.

Gut Health Champion

High in both soluble and insoluble fiber, mango promotes healthy digestion, feeds beneficial gut bacteria, and supports a balanced microbiome.

Anti-Inflammatory

Ubá mango pulp contains mangiferin — a powerful xanthone with proven anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties that protect cells from chronic damage.

Rich in Minerals

Potassium, calcium, iron, magnesium, phosphorus, and zinc — all found in Brazilian mango — support heart health, bone density, and hundreds of vital bodily functions.

The Brazil Story

From Minas Gerais to Your Bowl.

Brazil is now the largest mango producer in South America, harvesting over 900,000 tons of fruit each year — and the Ubá variety from Minas Gerais is among its most treasured exports.

The Zona da Mata region of Minas Gerais provides the ideal combination of altitude, rainfall, and rich soil that makes Ubá mangoes so exceptionally flavorful — conditions that can't be replicated anywhere else in the world.

When you eat a Pink Parrot mango bowl, you're tasting the result of centuries of agricultural tradition and the unique terroir of one of Brazil's most beloved fruit-growing regions.

The Ubá Variety

Why Ubá is Special.

Not all mangoes are created equal. The Ubá variety from Brazil is a native cultivar with a unique nutritional profile — scientifically confirmed to contain some of the highest concentrations of bioactive compounds of any mango variety in the world.

Origin

Native to the Minas Gerais region of Brazil — specifically the Zona da Mata — where ideal altitude and climate produce its signature sweetness.

Appearance

Small to medium-sized with a golden-yellow skin and characteristic reddish blush when perfectly ripe. Unmistakably beautiful.

Flavor Profile

Intensely sweet, rich, and aromatic with a fiberless, juicy flesh — considered by many to be the finest-tasting mango in South America.

Bioactive Compounds

Contains mangiferin, mangiferin gallate, isomangiferin gallate, kaempferol, and quercetin — a unique combination of powerful antioxidants confirmed by Brazilian researchers.
